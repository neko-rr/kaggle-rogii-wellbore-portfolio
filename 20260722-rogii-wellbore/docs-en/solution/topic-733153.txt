1. Effective sample size is the number of wells, not the number of rows

My final stack carried about twenty post-hoc constants 窶・per-band offsets, gain
weights, blend weights 窶・that were fitted against public leaderboard feedback.
I treated each measurement as exact because it was exact: it recovered the
public subset's mean to four decimal places.

What I never did was ask how well the public subset's mean estimates the
population mean.

My own analysis had already answered it. 63% of residual variance in this
problem is between-well. Rows within a well are one effective observation, not
three thousand. With ~199 hidden wells and roughly a quarter of them public,
that's ~55 effective samples:

SE(band mean) 竕・8 ft / 竏・5 竕・1.1 ft


The offsets I was shipping were 0.5窶・.5 ft. I was fitting noise of exactly
the size of my signal, and then stacking twenty of those corrections on top of
each other.

If you take one thing from this post: when between-group variance dominates,
your n is the group count. In this competition that single number 窶・55
instead of 180,000 窶・was the difference between a defensible correction and a
guaranteed collapse, and it was computable in week one without a single
submission.

2. Precision is not validity

I built a measurement procedure that predicted twenty consecutive submission
scores to within 0.001窶・.004 RMSE, five correction layers deep. That record was
the reason I kept going, and it was worthless as evidence.

It predicted the public score. The public score was the quantity being
fitted. Every "confirmation" was self-consistency, never generalisation 窶・a
perfectly calibrated instrument reporting, over and over, that it had
successfully hit the target I aimed it at.

An accuracy record only counts as validation if the thing being predicted is
independent of the thing being optimised. Mine wasn't, and I let the sheer
consistency of it override an argument I could have made from first principles.

3. The signal told me to throw away the only component that generalised

This is the part I find hardest to look at.

My best private submission (8.859) was one I rejected. It was an ANCC
spatial surface model 窶・a regional dip plane plus a Fourier-feature MLP on the
residual, fitted with GroupKFold by well 窶・blended in at weight 0.25.

Standalone it is a bad model: 13.5 RMSE against 9.4 for my best booster. But
it was by far the most decorrelated partner I ever measured, ﾏ・= 0.25窶・.56
against the tree ensemble where every other model family sat at 0.93窶・.95. It is
a different information source 窶・spatial geometry 窶・rather than another
learner on the same features.

The public leaderboard said its optimal blend weight was 0.026. I shrank it
to nothing and moved on. The private set wanted 0.25+. Every one of my top
five private submissions carries a higher field weight than public said was
optimal.

The mechanism is worth internalising: my public-fitted constants had already
absorbed the public residual, so a genuinely generalising component had nothing
left to explain there and looked useless. Overfitting doesn't just add
noise 窶・it actively suppresses the components that would have saved you.

4. A blend weight measured against a weak base does not transfer to a strong one

This one is general and I'd never seen it stated plainly. For a two-model blend,

w* = (ﾏダbﾂｲ 竏・ﾏ・ｷﾏダbﾂｷﾏダf) / (ﾏダbﾂｲ + ﾏダfﾂｲ 竏・2ﾏ・ｷﾏダbﾂｷﾏダf)


The numerator collapses as your own model improves. I measured the same spatial
partner at w* = 0.22 offline (against a sub-branch with ﾏダb = 9.51) and
w* = 0.026 on the leaderboard (against the composed final, ﾏダb = 6.12).
Same partner, same physics, same ﾏ・窶・the weight moved by 8ﾃ・ purely because the
base changed.

Corollary, which is what makes it useful: a partner is worth most where you
are weakest. Re-derive every blend weight at the ﾏダb it will actually ship
against, and expect it to be band-dependent.

5. A partner's error magnitude transfers; its correlation with your error does not

Two separate partners confirmed this. The spatial field's ﾏ・transferred from
offline to hidden within 2% (14.04 竊・13.75). A gamma-ray alignment
estimator's RMS transferred within 8.5%. Both were correctly built.

But their correlation with my error evaporated. The GR estimator regressed at
ﾏ・= +0.274 offline and returned +0.026 on the hidden set 窶・not weakened,
absent.

The reason is structural, and I made the same mistake twice: I measured both
partners against one branch of my model and shipped them against the composed
final. Correlation with your error is a property of your base, not of the
partner. The GR offset looked like new information against the learned branch
alone; against a prediction that was already 37% a spatial projection, it was
redundant.

Validate any new information source against the final composed prediction,
never against one component of it.

7. An offline model comparison is only valid if the baseline is what you're actually replacing

I shipped two model swaps and both failed on the leaderboard. The second one
took nine days of feature work 窶・pruning 41 harmful features, adding spatial
ones, a retrained ensemble 窶・and looked like +0.61 ft offline.

The flaw: I compared my retrain of the incumbent's config against my new
models. But what actually ships is the incumbent's real artifacts, for
which no honest OOF exists and cannot exist 窶・they were trained on all of my
training wells. I had measured "new config beats my reproduction of their
config" and deployed it as if it were "new models beat their models."

If the incumbent can't be scored honestly on your CV, you have no offline read
on the swap at all.

8. When an offline number would leapfrog you 90 ranks, assume leakage

Four separate self-leak traps, three of them in code I had already written and
believed:



an LOO benchmark whose fallback averaged the query well's own points

evaluating models on the three visible test wells 窶・which are also present in
train/ with full targets

refitting both models before comparing, so the delta was dominated by the
difference in leak rather than skill (this one predicted a jump from ~95th to
top 5)

a local neighbour-distance reproduction where the leaked copy of a well filled
every neighbour slot, silently collapsing the analysis to zero rows


Implausibility was a better detector than any code review. The reflex worth
building: a result this good is evidence of a bug, not of skill.



What I'd do differently



Compute effective sample size before fitting anything. Groups, not rows.
It kills most fine-grained corrections on arithmetic alone.

Cap the number of parameters fitted against any signal you can't hold out
窶・against n_eff, not against your row count.

Shrink hard. Anything whose only measurement channel is the leaderboard
ships at a fraction of its measured optimum, or not at all.

Select final entries by offline CV, not public score. My best private
submission was already in my history. I just didn't pick it.

When an offline-validated component and the leaderboard disagree, the
leaderboard is not automatically right. I'd adopted "offline gates screen,
the LB decides" as a rule after it worked twice early on. It was the wrong
rule and it cost the competition.


An exact instrument aimed at the wrong target takes you exactly where you
pointed it. Happy to discuss any of this 窶・and if anyone in the top 20 measured
the public/private transfer of their own stack, I'd genuinely like to compare.

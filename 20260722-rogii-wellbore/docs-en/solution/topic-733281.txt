Manual matching soft

For starsteer I decided I want to do something similar and vibecoded manual well matching software. You could upload a well with a rank based on how well constant predicts it (1 = worst prediction), look at it gr, tvt, typewell gr, selfreverse gr, metrics and try to choose a window size, scale, smooth, offset yourself to match the signals. 



Looks like a nice and easy match, ye (the right small image is the horizontall well + prediction)? But in reality it was easy to match only "strong" movements TVT, when "slight" (which were the most) were barely recognizable.

Breakthrough

PF

About the middle of July I decided that my approach is the dead end. Also a lot of problem understanding came from @shreygandhi early writeup, tbh it was the only one I read because he was top 6 at that time.

So I decided to switch to PF, here actual understanding of the pattern matching helped in many cases. My approach was designing different types of PF's. How to choose PF's for blend

At the moment I had already about 30 PF's, running full candidates estimators would not be possible anymore. From @shreygandhi writeup I took the idea of estimating oracle-type metric and adding PF's in a greedy way.

Ranker

I decided that simple blend should be replaced with ranker supervised model to choose which PF to use for each segment.

Features beyond the PF bank

The 20 PF trajectories were supplemented with several groups of inference-time features. Different selector models used different subsets:



Trajectory dynamics: slope, displacement and local variation.

PF disagreement: spread and distance between candidates.

GR matching: agreement with type-well and observed GR.

Well geometry: depth, vertical movement and relative position.

Surface context: agreement with nearby geological surfaces.

Temporal context: changes between consecutive well segments.

Spatial reliability: historical PF performance in nearby wells.

Whole-well context: global statistics and early-to-late drift.


Performance-based priors were computed from training-fold wells only.

How the selector worked

Each model scored the 20 PF candidates over well segments. The scores were converted into blending weights and interpolated along the well. It was done to have a smooth prediction and avoid heavy errors done due to the incorrect selection.

Yuto's part 窶・Synthetic-pretrained U-Net

This is the U-Net leg of our 8th place solution. The final submission was 0.5 ﾃ・b230_cpu20 (@bluepill) + 0.5 ﾃ・residual_spatial5, and the latter is my 5-fold U-Net bank, which @sggpls extended with his tail-25 residual correction and ms7000 spatial folds. What follows covers the U-Net itself only 窶・the residual correction, the fold definitions, the blend, and the PF side are all other members' work.

TL;DR



The error is per-well dip lock-in, not tail difficulty. The last 25% of a well accounts for 41.6% of total SSE, but only because dip error accumulates. Oracle-removing a per-well linear trend takes 6.44 竊・3.33.

A synthetic generator that invents the geological surface u = TVT + Z gave zero-shot 7.62 after 200 epochs that never touch a real well. Inventing TVT directly is caught by a domain gate at AUC 1.0.

Dropping pretrain 竊・finetune in favour of keeping 50% synthetic flowing throughout real-data training broke the 6.6 wall and reached 5.06.

The pretraining seed is a lottery, and the pretraining score cannot screen it. Re-rolling took a single fold from 9.081 竊・6.496.

The same recipe fails on different wells when you change the fold split (residual correlation 0.826). Mixing in 30% of a group scheme that is 0.41 worse on average beats stratified alone.

Synthetic data: inventing the geological surface

The training data has 773 wells, so each fold of a 5-fold split trains on roughly 618 of them. An image CNN does not learn "matching" 窶・it memorises a vocabulary of geological surfaces. My initial generator only rescaled real surfaces, so each fold's model saw the same ~618 surfaces every epoch.

Inventing TVT directly failed. I implemented a domain gate that discriminates real from synthetic, and it separated them at AUC 1.0 using surface curvature alone.

The cause is physical. Drillers steer Z to follow the formation, which couples dTVT 竕・-dZ. Inventing TVT alone breaks that coupling and roughens the surface. But inventing the surface u = TVT + Z completely independently is no better: with the real trajectory fixed, the wellbore drifts ~100 ft off the typewell and stops being coherent geosteering.

The construction that worked is u_new = S_real + d.

On top of that, decoy_prob = 0.25 injects a false match at ﾂｱ15 ft. That is the Eagle Ford aliasing period, so this is adversarial training against the dominant failure mode 窶・locking onto an adjacent bed.

The real trajectory and prefix annotations stay real; only the horizontal GR is re-rendered along the new path, preserving per-row observation residuals and missingness patterns.

Training with synth_prob=1 (never showing a real well) for 200 epochs gives zero-shot 7.62.

Variance from the pretraining seed

One fold sat 2 ft worse than the others (9.081). Re-rolling only the pretraining seed brought it to 6.496 (竏・.59) 窶・same recipe, same statistics, same validation wells.

The problem is that you cannot identify a good seed from its pretraining score.

The two are uncorrelated. The noise floor is stage-dependent: ImageNet-initialised pretraining spans 0.955 ft at ep10 for the same seed, while mixed continuation spans 0.008 ft. Almost all the variance comes from the pretraining draw.

Two practical takeaways. Do not screen seeds by pretraining score. If a fold is 2+ ft worse than the others, suspect an outlier draw rather than a hard fold.

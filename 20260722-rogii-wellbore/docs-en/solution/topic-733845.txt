1. Big picture: a multi-path, decorrelated ensemble

The single most important lesson: even when you have one strong model, uncorrelated experts still buy you a lot.
My matcher (grtx) alone was already gold-zone on the public LB (5.08). But it didn't stop mattering once
I bolted on other paths that make their mistakes in different places.

Three paths, three mechanisms:



grtx 窶・the main force. A single-well GR竊杯ypewell iterative-registration transformer. Pure single-well
info: no neighbor wells, no kriging. This is where most of the score comes from.

Physics path 窶・pfnet + pftree. A GPU particle filter as a physical base, plus neural / tree models
that learn the residual on top. GR + spatial prior fused the "physics" way.

Geometric path 窶・ancc (kriging) + geotx. No GR at all 窶・pure geometry / neighbor-well geology
extrapolation. Weak alone, but orthogonal to the GR matcher.


The blend is a simple per-point weighted sum, with grtx absorbing the residual weight:

out = (1 竏・w_pf 竏・w_ancc 竏・w_geotx) ﾂｷ grtx
      + w_ancc ﾂｷ ancc  +  w_geotx ﾂｷ geotx      竊・geometric path
      + w_pf   ﾂｷ (pfnet / pftree)              竊・physics path


The one non-obvious bit: the geometric path uses distance-varying weights.
w = W / (1 + (nd/D)ﾂｲ), where nd = distance from that point to the nearest neighbor-well ANCC control point.



In dense well areas (nd small) 竊・the geometric legs get real weight (neighbor geology is trustworthy).

For isolated wells (nd large) 竊・the weight decays to ~0 (don't trust extrapolation).

ancc decays fast (D竕・50窶・00), geotx decays slow (D竕・200窶・500): ancc is strong up close, geotx in the
mid-range, crossover around nd竕・00.

pfnet/pftree use fixed weights 窶・per-well / per-point gating looked great on CV but died on the LB
(the "selection wall", see ﾂｧ6).




2. grtx 窶・the main force (models referred to as grtx-XXX)

This is a GR-matching problem: the horizontal-well (hw) GR log is a distorted, shifted copy of the
typewell (tw) GR profile, and we want the vertical drift (= ﾎ尿NCC 竏・ﾎ濃) at every point. So grtx is
basically a learned image-registration model between the two 1-D logs (hw and tw).

2.1 Data &amp; feature handling



CV strategy: plain 5-fold random (GroupKFold by well). Every LB submission is the mean of all 5 folds.

Dimension choices that mattered:


horizontal GR compressed to 16 ft/sample (COMPRESSION=16) 窶・12 ft and 24 ft were both worse.

N=768 samples per horizontal well; typewell resampled to S=320 states 窶・everything is indexed
relative to the anchor (the known竊弾val transition: drift = 0 there, and the sinh state grid is centered on it).

INPUT_CLIP=80: only keep the known section within anchorﾂｱ80 ft. Far-away known data is uncorrelated and
actually hurts.

sinh non-uniform state grid (the key trick). The drift distribution is a tight funnel 窶・most mass is
within a few ft of the anchor, but the tail reaches ﾂｱ100+ ft. A uniform ﾂｱ60 grid both wastes resolution
in the dense center and fails to cover the tail. Instead I lay states on g = Aﾂｷsinh(bﾂｷt): ~0.26 ft/cell
in the center (fine where the mass is), stretching out to ﾂｱ115 ft in the tail (full coverage). It's
analytically invertible (closed-form asinh to find a cell), so no binary search.



Left竊池ight: a uniform grid vs the sinh grid; how the sinh cells map to drift; and why 窶・cell resolution is
spent exactly where the drift probability mass is.



Per-well joint robust norm: hw and tw are concatenated and normalized by one shared median/MAD.
This keeps the hw竊杯w gain relationship intact while removing per-pair offset/scale (which would otherwise
let the model memorize well identity).


2.2 Architecture: shared encoder 竊・transformer 竊・RAFT



Shared encoder (hw &amp; tw). Both logs go through the same conv stem (shared weights). hw carries two
channels [GR, ﾎ濃]; the typewell is clean GR only, so it's given an all-zero ﾎ濃 channel to match the
2-channel shape 窶・then the identical encoder applies to both. Sharing matters: separate per-log stems made
CV worse 窶・the shared embedding is what keeps hw and tw in the same feature space so they can be matched.

Transformer contextualizer (5窶・ layers, dim 192窶・56). GR is not injective 窶・the same little wiggle
appears in many places, so local matching is ambiguous. Global attention is what disambiguates.

Grounded readout. A cross-attention produces a correspondence/cost map C; drift = C @ ref_drift.
C is directly supervised to peak at the true drift state (CE_W=10), and regularized with a 2-D conv over
the cost map (stereo/optical-flow style).

RAFT iterative refinement. A 1-D adaptation of RAFT (Teed &amp; Deng, RAFT: Recurrent All-Pairs Field
Transforms for Optical Flow, ECCV 2020 窶・arXiv:2003.12039): a
correlation pyramid (7 levels, ﾂｱ4-tap lookups each round that together cover the whole ﾂｱ115 ft state axis)


ConvGRU updates + convex upsampling, iterating the drift estimate. Deployment uses the last RAFT iteration.



hw and tw go through a shared conv stem + transformer 竊・cost volume; a grounded readout turns it into drift.
A separate context encoder (fed by hw) produces the h/inp that drive the RAFT loop, which iteratively
refines the drift.

Training loss 窶・both heads are supervised (everything on the eval segment):



Readout head: a cross-entropy that peaks the cost volume C at the true drift state (weight 10 窶・the
dominant term), plus a Huber on the readout drift (weight 0.2). This is what shapes a clean cost volume.

RAFT: an L1 on every iteration's drift, exponentially up-weighting later rounds
(ﾎ｣ ﾎｳ^(K竏段)ﾂｷL1, ﾎｳ=0.8, weight 1.0) 窶・the RAFT sequence loss.

Plus a small Huber "de-damping" term that pushes the expectation toward the true drift.


Deploy / OOF uses the RAFT last iteration; the readout is a training-time head only.

How much does RAFT actually buy? The deployed models run 24 RAFT iterations at inference. In tuning,
~16 was already the sweet spot 窶・beyond that it flatlines (24 is a hair worse, well within noise) 窶・so the exact
count past 16 barely matters. Comparing the readout (pre-RAFT) against the RAFT output on the grtx-341 OOF:
RAFT improves 524 of 773 wells and hurts 249, taking the pooled RMSE from 7.55 竊・5.94. The wells it
hurts are the hard ones (the spurious-warp wells of ﾂｧ6) 窶・but the ~2.4 ft it gains on the bulk far outweighs
the ~1.4 ft it gives back on the tail.


Per-well RMSE, readout vs RAFT (grtx-341). Left: points below the diagonal are wells RAFT improves (green, 524)
vs wells it hurts (red, 249). Right: the per-well ﾎ・distribution 窶・mostly negative (RAFT better), pooled
7.55 竊・5.94.

2.3 The data engine: three parallel loaders

773 wells is tiny for a matcher this expressive 窶・it overfits and plateaus. The fix is a data engine made
of three parallel loaders, mixed live in every batch (the DataLoader workers regenerate the synthetic
ones on the fly, so the model basically never sees the same well twice):



real wells + heavy on-the-fly augmentation,

whip 窶・synthetic drift trajectories,

layer 窶・bulk formation shifts.


Loaders 2 and 3 share a forward-GR simulator (the "inversion" function) that I'll describe at the end.

Loader 1 窶・real wells + augmentation



Landing-aware anchor slide (this is a bigger deal than it sounds). The known/eval split isn't fixed 窶・
every epoch it's resampled anywhere from the well's landing point outward along the horizontal. So one real
well becomes many training examples with different anchor positions and known-section lengths. It's the
cheapest, highest-value augmentation here.



Each panel is one well's TVT vs MD. Red = landing point, orange = the "real" anchor split, green band = the
range the anchor is randomly sampled from during training. The split slides freely across the landed
horizontal section.



Typewell warp (WARP_P=0.8) 窶・deform the typewell's layer thicknesses and sync-distort the hw GR
(drift/geometry unchanged): texture diversity so the matcher doesn't memorize exact log shapes.

ANCC shape-library swap (SHAPE_AUG_P=0.8) 窶・replace the well's ANCC shape with one drawn from a
library of training-fold shapes (ﾎｱ-blend, variance-preserving) 竊・a new drift shape on the same geometry.



Shape swap on 6 wells (real vs swapped drift) and the distribution of drift-shape change across wells.

Plus optional kink injection: hard wells are dominated by sharp formation kinks whose natural base rate
  is only 2窶・%, so we inject more 窶・with a steering response where the wellbore ﾎ濃 lags and follows the
  bend (real wells track kinks at ~0.5 ratio), so the synthetic trajectory doesn't pretend to be blind.


The ANCC (formation-depth) curve for 4 wells: blue = real (笆ｼ marks its natural kink), orange dashed = the
same well with a synthetic kink injected (笘・. We deliberately add these sharp bends because they're the #1
hard-well failure mode and rare in the raw data.

Loader 2 窶・whip (synthetic drift)

Take a real well and replace its eval-segment drift with a pinned random walk that fans out funnel-style,
deliberately over-populating the tail that real wells rarely reach. Then re-simulate the GR (below).


Left: real 773-well drift (narrow funnel). Middle: whip synthetic (wider 窶・fills the tail). Right: the
max|drift| distributions, showing whip covering the region real wells under-sample.

Loader 3 窶・layer shift

Shift the whole well up/down 竊・the GR samples a different formation level, while drift/ﾎ濃 stay fixed.
Orthogonal to whip (whip changes the shape of drift; layer changes which formation you're logging).


Layer shift on 6 wells (real vs shifted GR) and the applied-shift distribution.

The forward / inversion GR simulator (shared by loaders 2 &amp; 3)

Whenever the drift (whip) or the formation level (layer) changes, the GR that the horizontal well would have
logged has to be regenerated 窶・otherwise the (GR, drift) pair is inconsistent. That's this function, and
getting it realistic is what makes the synthetic wells actually help:



sample the typewell along anchor + drift 竊・the clean GR (what a perfectly-tracking well would see);

add vertical mismatch 窶・a TVT-persistent field (same depth 竊・same distortion), amplitude scaled to the
well's signal, with per-well random severity, calibrated to the real 773-well statistics (it's a vertical
mismatch, not a horizontal warp 窶・real logs line up at lag 0);

add speckle noise;

sparsify + interpolate to mimic the gaps in real GR logs.



Top: the simulator steps. Bottom: a real example 窶・clean typewell sampling (blue) turned into a realistic
synthetic GR (red) with mismatch, noise and gaps.


  
Counter-intuitive lesson: "looks more realistic" 竕 "trains better." A visually gorgeous steep-heel
  synthetic variant actually made CV worse. Trust the CV, not your eyes.




3. Physics path: pfnet + pftree (pfnet-XXX, pftree-XXX)

3.1 GPU-ifying the particle filter (gen_gpupf)

The classic geosteering tool is a particle filter over the ANCC state. The public/CPU PF is slow and weak.
I rewrote it as one GPU function gen_gpupf(Z, **10 params) 竊・{well: drift}, used identically for
training-base generation and kernel inference. Ten knobs (seeds, particles, momentum ﾎｱ, SDE motion,
emission width, kriging geo-prior weight w_geo, readout mode窶ｦ) generate every PF "expert" from one code path.
The deployed base (gpupf_g3geo_krig) runs with gs_mult=3, ﾎｱ=0.998, w_geo=0.05, 64 seeds ﾃ・500
particles, SDE motion 窶・a raw OOF 9.77, versus ~14.35 for the competition's built-in PF. That gap is
most of the physics path's value. (w_geo here is the PF's internal spatial-prior weight 窶・gs_mult is the
emission width, ﾎｱ the dip momentum 窶・not to be confused with the ancc/geotx blend weights in ﾂｧ5.)

3.2 pfnet architecture

A two-branch net on top of the PF base:



a 1-D branch learning the residual on the PF base drift,

a 2-D branch (UNet over the g3 particle-trajectory image),

plus a formation-kriging "combo" channel.


The philosophy is residual-on-a-strong-base, which mirrors the physics: the PF base gives the big picture,
the net fixes the local errors. With twin (forward-sim) augmentation on top, single-model ~8.67.


The GPU particle-filter base carries the big picture; a 1-D branch learns the residual, a 2-D UNet reads the
particle-trajectory image, and a formation-kriging channel adds spatial geology 窶・summed back onto the base.

3.3 pftree 窶・a tabular residual model, and its feature study

A per-point gradient-boosting model (XGBoost + depth-diverse CatBoost 竊・ridge stack) predicting drift from a
~195-feature bank 窶・the "everything a single well knows about this point" set. The families, with examples:



Neighbor-geology kriging 窶・the ANCC formation surface (and a handful of related surfaces) kriged in from
neighboring wells at this X/Y (leave-one-out), plus a distance/reliability feature. This is the geometric
prior 窶・where the formation should be based on the neighbors.

GR-matching descriptors 窶・match the hw GR against the typewell at several stiffnesses/windows and read
off the implied drift and how much those estimates agree (disagreement = low-confidence point).

Particle-filter outputs 窶・the gpupf drift and its spread.

Local GR &amp; geometry 窶・rolling GR statistics, ﾎ濃, heading, distance-from-anchor.


The single biggest lever was residual-on-a-strong-base: instead of predicting drift directly, put the
gpupf PF drift underneath and let the trees learn only the residual (~1.5 ft single-model jump).



4. Geometric path



ancc 窶・spatial IDW kriging of the ANCC formation surface (K60, leave-one-out). Base drift cv ~14.6.

geotx 窶・a GR-free geometric transformer (d=160, 5 layers, 8 heads, ~1.6M params). Input = a 768-point
resampled well with 9 channels (known-drift history, is-known mask, ﾎ濃, dx, dy, heading cos/sin,
grid-kriging neighbor geology, nn-dist reliability); it outputs drift directly. Trained with sliding-window
anchor augmentation (the key regularizer against 773-well overfitting), 5 folds averaged. Single cv ~11.3.


Both are weak alone (11窶・4 ft 窶・geology has an irreducible part that geometry simply can't see without GR).
But their mechanism is completely orthogonal to the GR matcher, so in the blend they punch far above their
single-model weight.



5. CV vs LB, and the submission strategy (the part that actually won gold)

5.1 Score progression (all public LB 窶・but public LB lies at small scale, refer to ﾂｧ5.2)



single grtx: 5.08

+ geometric path (ancc): 竊・5.03

+ physics path (pfnet): 竊・4.90


The core rule: decorrelation &gt; single-model strength. The cleanest proof: a residual-on-pfnet tree had a
stronger single model (8.44) but was 0.96-correlated with pfnet 竊・zero blend gain. Meanwhile a weak
GR-free geometric leg (single model only 11窶・4) bought 竏・.4 in the blend. Adding an uncorrelated leg beats
strengthening an existing one, every time.

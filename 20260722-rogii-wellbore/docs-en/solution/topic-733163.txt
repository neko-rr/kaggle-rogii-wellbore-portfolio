2. Physical framing

Everything follows from one geometric identity:

TVT = FE 竏・Z          (formation elevation minus borehole depth)


Z is surveyed and available in test. So the unknown is a single smooth curve
FE(MD), not 5000 independent TVT values. The problem collapses from "predict a trajectory" to "estimate one
slowly-varying rate a = dFE/dMD; geometry supplies the shape."

Two consequences that shaped everything:



The shape of the prediction is largely given by Z already. GR's only job is calibrating the rate.

4. Three first principles

Derived empirically; each killed a whole family of ideas.

P1 窶・Equal SNR across frequency and space. Any mechanism that re-weights existing evidence fails: filtering,
multi-scale, band selection, despiking, adaptive gs, heteroscedastic weighting. All tested, all worse.

P2 窶・Over-constraint is regularisation (the "1/3 law"). Optimal hyperparameters are systematically ~1/3 of the
measured statistic: shrink
0.79 竊・0.30, SIG_RATE 0.0078 竊・0.002, SIG_DELTA 2.1 竊・0.75 ft. Eleven independent relaxation attempts all made
things worse.

P3 窶・Sparse blanks are a third layer of regularisation. Densifying the template (interpolation, lower thresholds,
coarser grid, kernel smoothing) makes it statistically better (ﾎｲ 0.79 竊・0.83) and the DP worse (6.21 竊・6.53).
Lowering shrink does not compensate.

Read rows 2 and 3 together 窶・this is the whole story of the competition.

The reference curve (the "typewell") is a vertical well's GR log used as a template for matching. It doesn't perfectly
describe the rock at the horizontal well's location, so we build a correction from the part of the well where we
already know the answer. Row 2 says: if that correction were perfect everywhere, we'd score 3.99. Row 3 says: if it's
perfect only in the depth range the known part of the well actually passed through, we score 6.04 窶・barely better
than my real 6.205.

In other words: the correction we can actually estimate is already estimated about as well as it can be. The missing
2.2 ft lives entirely in depths the well never visited before going blind. A frequency decomposition showed that
missing information sits below 16 ft wavelength 窶・fine detail that simply is not present in the vertical well's log at
all.

That is an information wall, not an engineering problem. After measuring this I should have stopped trying to improve
the single model.

Row 4 is the other surprise: giving the model the true average dip angle makes it worse. The model's hand-tuned
constraints are calibrated around its own imperfect estimate; handing it a better number breaks that calibration. This
is the first sign of a theme that runs through everything below 窶・being more faithful to reality does not mean
predicting better.

6. The core impasse 窶・why the model sometimes locks onto the wrong layer

The failure mode. Sedimentary rock is layered and cyclic: sand-shale-sand, repeating. So the GR log looks similar
at depths roughly 12 ft apart. When the model matches the horizontal well's GR against the reference curve, it can lock
onto the wrong repeat 窶・off by one "floor" of the building. Once locked, it stays locked and follows that wrong layer
for thousands of feet.

The evidence for this diagnosis:



Errors are staircase-shaped: a 4-step piecewise-constant fit covers 95.5% of the well within 3 ft. Errors don't
drift, they jump.

The typical jump is 7.48 ft, and the typewell's own repeat spacing (measured by autocorrelation) has a median of
12.5 ft, with 54% of wells in the 8窶・5 ft range. Same scale. The jumps go to the neighbouring repeat. Why it
cannot be fixed by searching harder. The algorithm I use (forward窶澱ackward) is exact 窶・it computes the
mathematically correct answer given the model. So the model isn't failing to find the best explanation; the best
explanation is genuinely wrong. Measured at the jump points:


cost of taking the correct path (from the smoothness assumption):   30窶・9 units
how much better the GR data fits the correct path:                  竏・.14 units


That second number is negative. The GR data itself very slightly prefers the wrong layer. No search improvement can
fix that 窶・it's a statement about the data.

Why I couldn't detect it either. If I can't prevent the mistake, maybe I can at least flag it and fall back to
something safe. So I built 13 different detectors 窶・posterior uncertainty, disagreement between forward and backward
passes, evidence scores, disagreement across model variants, gradient-boosted trees on geometry, a CNN on the sequence,
and several more. Every one performed at chance level.

The most damning measurement: I looked at how concentrated the errors are along each well. In wells that went badly
wrong, the worst 10% of segments account for 34.7% of the total error. In wells that went fine, that number is
34.6%. Identical. Catastrophic wells are not a different kind of failure 窶・they are the same process, just with
bigger amplitude. There is no boundary to detect.

7. What failed

This is the part I'd most want to read in someone else's writeup, so here it is in full. The short version: I tried
about twenty distinct mechanisms; exactly one (the ensemble) worked. Everything below has a number attached, and most
entries close off a whole family of ideas rather than one experiment.

A recurring lesson worth stating up front: several of these failed precisely because they made the model more
physically realistic. The model is deliberately too rigid, and that rigidity is what stops it from bending its
trajectory to fit the wrong rock layer.

7a. The dip angle ("rate") 窶・four ways to attack it, four dead ends

"Rate" here means how fast the well is moving up or down through the rock layers. It's the one number GR is supposed to
calibrate.



Give it the true average value 竊・6.569, worse than estimating it (above).

Take away its freedom to vary 竊・11.083. Catastrophic; the well must be allowed to deviate.

Tune how much it's allowed to wander 竊・sharp optimum at 0.002. Interesting detail: 0.001 (8.445) is worse than
0.003 (6.987). Being too rigid hurts more than being too loose 窶・the only place in this project where that's true.

Change the shape of the wandering. I measured the real distribution of rate changes: it has kurtosis 1226 and
sd/MAD = 49.9 窶・wildly heavy-tailed, meaning most steps are tiny but occasionally there's a huge jump (faults,
structural breaks). This is real, present in 40% of wells. So I replaced the bell-curve assumption with heavy-tailed
alternatives that match reality: Laplace (7.008), a two-Gaussian mixture (6.74 / 6.97 / 7.14), rare full resets, and
jumps triggered by wellbore curvature (6.21 / 6.34). Every single one made the score worse. Modelling reality
faithfully made prediction worse, because heavy tails give the trajectory permission to jump 窶・and it jumps to the
wrong layer.


7b. Using the well's XY position

Formation surfaces tilt in 2D, so in principle the well's horizontal heading matters. In practice: only 5.8% of wells
turn more than 15ﾂｰ, fitting a tilted plane and extrapolating is catastrophic (126 ft error vs 26 ft on the turning
wells), and the correlation between "how sharply the well curves" and "how much the rock dip changes" is 0.044. The
wells are nearly straight; there's no information in the heading.

7c. Improving the reference curve



Stretching the depth axis (layers thicken/thin laterally, so the vertical well's depth scale may not match the
horizontal well's). This is real 窶・spread of about ﾂｱ2%, and estimable from the known part of the well. But correcting
it improves the GR match by only 1.8%, far too little to matter.

Finer depth resolution. The typewell is sampled every 0.5 ft. Coarsening it to 1.0 ft costs 0.70 ft of score, so
the model is genuinely sensitive here 窶・but there is no finer data available, and finer model grid (0.25 ft) made
things worse, not better.

Coverage, not accuracy, is the constraint 窶・see ﾂｧ5.

Instead of matching GR values, match shapes: find peak窶鍍rough窶菟eak patterns in the horizontal well's GR and look for
the same pattern in the reference curve. This gives a sparse set of hard pins 窶・"at this point in the well, the depth is
here, ﾂｱ1 ft."

It works remarkably well where it fires: matching reaches 97.1% top-1 at 2.9% false rate when the query curve is
built on truth TVT. Crucially, replacing truth with the DP prediction barely degrades it (88.9% vs 89.1%) 窶・shape
matching is invariant to a constant delta offset 窶・and wrong wells score identically to correct wells (0.959 vs

have any counterpart in the typewell; the best usable rule reaches 61.6% precision at ﾂｱ2 ft; soft-masking it into the DP
hurt monotonically as the mask hardened (6.167 / 6.171 / 6.196 / 6.207). Geometric consistency voting failed to filter
false anchors because typewell self-similarity makes same-well errors correlated 窶・multiple anchors drift into the
same secondary basin together.

7f. Fitting a smooth curve instead of a step-by-step path

The trajectory is extremely compressible (cheat measurement:
deg1 6.699, deg3 4.425, deg4 3.820 窶・a straight line already matches the DP). But CMA-ES reaches only 17.6, and
100% of wells have a global optimum with lower cost than the truth coefficients (0.849 vs 1.685; two independent
runs converge identically). A smooth trajectory can slice diagonally across multiple horizontal low-cost bands, placing
every step in some local minimum; the truth is confined to one basin and must pass through high-cost regions.
Parameterisation blocks per-block jumps but grants a worse freedom: continuous traversal of multiple basins. This is
precisely why the DP's per-block Markov structure plus rate prior wins 窶・it forces local coherence.

10. What I would do differently

Neighbour-well bedding-plane priors. I closed this axis because only 21 of 773 wells share a typewell file 窶・but
that measures shared typewell, not spatial proximity. Another competitor independently reached CV竕・B 8.0 using
XY-neighbour bedding planes to estimate g' = dFE/dMD and reported it works well. My xyz_diag tested something
different ("does XY azimuth help the DP's rate?", corr 0.044) and I over-generalised the negative result. This is the
one axis I believe was closed in error.

Accept the ceiling earlier. TPL_COV_ONLY = 6.041 窶・perfect template values in observable bins are worth only 0.16
ft 窶・was measurable early. It should have redirected effort to cross-well information immediately, instead of the ~10
further single-model mechanisms I tested afterwards.

Measure before building. The habits that paid off: every new parameter's off-value must reproduce the baseline
bit-for-bit before sweeping; pass/fail criteria declared before running; oracle probes to bound the ceiling before
implementing; null controls whenever taking a minimum over estimators. These caught at least four wrong conclusions and
three patches that had silently never taken effect.

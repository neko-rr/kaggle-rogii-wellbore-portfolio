TL;DR



The decisive layer was Bayesian: three complementary GR posteriors handled alignment ambiguity, posterior-mode uncertainty and scale sensitivity.

The boosting base was CatBoost-only: no LightGBM or XGBoost was blended into the selected supervised branch.

Particle-filter alignment, trajectory geometry and spatial formation structure supplied strong priors.

A confidence model decided how far to move from the learned base toward a physical reconstruction for each well.

The Bayesian GR update was intentionally small: its maximum possible move was only ﾂｱ4.5 ft, with an automatic fallback to the unchanged prior.

The numerically best aggregate CV policy was not selected. Its advantage over the pre-validated policy was inside bootstrap uncertainty and did not reproduce consistently across folds.


1. Problem framing: estimate a geological trajectory, not independent rows

For each horizontal well, we observe:



the trajectory (MD, X, Y, Z);

gamma ray GR along the horizontal well, with missing and noisy values;

TVT_input up to the Prediction Start point;

a paired vertical typewell containing GR(TVT).


We must infer the hidden continuation of TVT after the Prediction Start. The natural latent variable is therefore a smooth, well-level TVT path. The typewell GR curve is a noisy geological dictionary: a candidate TVT path is plausible when the GR sampled along that path resembles the observed horizontal GR.

This framing shaped every part of the solution:



generate target-free alignment paths;

learn residual errors with well-grouped validation;

constrain the result with geometry and regional structure;

use GR again as a conservative posterior correction.


2. Validation was part of the model

The most important design choice was the validation unit. Random row splits would leak almost the entire shape of a held-out well into training, so all main comparisons used five folds grouped by well.

3. Stage 1: target-free particle alignment

The first prior came from aligning each horizontal GR sequence to its paired typewell GR curve.

For each well we ran:



500 particles per trajectory simulation;

32 deterministic seeds;

sequential GR likelihood weighting;

resampling when the effective particle count became too small.


The state was expressed through U = Z + TVT, which makes geological offset easier to model along the trajectory. The filter was anchored at the last visible TVT_input point and propagated into the blind interval.

Instead of trusting one stochastic path, we summarized the seed ensemble with robust statistics:



likelihood-weighted and per-step-weighted paths;

point-wise median and trimmed mean;

seed standard deviation, IQR and MAD;

effective sample size and entropy;

end-of-well disagreement and outlier diagnostics.


These quantities became both a target-free TVT prior and uncertainty features for the learned residual model.

4. Stage 2: CatBoost learned the particle-filter residual

The supervised base predicted the residual relative to the particle expert:

$$\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{Cat}}(i)=\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{PF}}(i)+f_{\mathrm{CatBoost}}(x_i).$$

The final model used 27 features drawn from four groups:



particle-path estimates and disagreements;

slopes from the visible TVT prefix;

trajectory coordinates and deltas;

azimuth, distance-from-anchor and likelihood diagnostics.


This was Cat-only in the boosting sense. ExtraTrees models appeared later only as well-level confidence and blend controllers; the selected boosting prediction did not ensemble CatBoost with LightGBM or XGBoost.

5. Heel-anchored geometry and regional physical structure

5.1 Heel-anchored projection

Unconstrained point-wise residuals can create an implausible jump immediately after the last known TVT. We therefore fitted a robust quadratic curve in

$$U=Z+\mathrm{TVT}.$$

as a function of normalized progress through the blind interval. The curve was fixed to zero offset at the heel, and the projected path was blended 50/50 with the raw CatBoost path.

5.2 Structural field

We constructed a second, independent physical estimate from regional information:



smoothed formation surfaces for EGFDU, EGFDL and BUDA;

a five-seed Fourier-feature neural potential field over (X, Y);

triangulated formation offsets;

cross-fitted residual profiles from geometrically similar wells.


The physical offset was:

$$\Delta U_{\mathrm{phys}}=0.25\,\Delta U_{\mathrm{potential}}+0.75\left(\Delta U_{\mathrm{surface}}+0.5\,\Delta U_{\mathrm{neighbor}}\right).$$

A small ExtraTrees committee estimated an initial per-well blend coefficient. A second confidence model then adjusted it using path-shape and multi-scale GR-fit diagnostics. Small predicted shifts below 0.03 were suppressed, shifts reached full confidence at 0.10, and full-confidence shifts were strengthened by 1.15. The final coefficient remained clipped to [0, 1].

We call the resulting branch Confidence-V2:

$$\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{V2}}=\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{Cat}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{well}}\left(\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{phys}}-\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{Cat}}\right).$$

6. The decisive layer: a three-scale Bayesian GR posterior

The final step revisited the core inverse problem: does the observed GR provide enough evidence to move the V2 trajectory? Instead of asking a deterministic aligner for one best path, we treated Confidence-V2 as the prior trajectory, the horizontal/typewell GR match as the likelihood, and a bounded correction curve as the posterior update.

The figure below shows the complete Bayesian update. The main idea is deliberately simple: preserve multiple plausible alignment modes, average across three GR scales, and cap how much the posterior is allowed to overrule the prior.



6.1 Why a posterior instead of one deterministic alignment?

Three properties of the data made a Bayesian formulation useful:



Repeated GR motifs create multiple plausible alignments. Keeping several posterior modes is safer than committing to one local optimum.

The evidence quality changes by well. Missing GR, weak typewell similarity and noisy intervals should reduce the confidence of the update.

The V2 path is already strong. The posterior should make a calibrated geological adjustment, not rebuild the global trajectory from scratch.


This distinction was central to the final solution: Bayesian inference was used as an uncertainty-aware decision layer, not as an unconstrained replacement model.

6.2 Robust observation model

The visible prefix was used to fit a robust affine mapping between typewell GR and horizontal GR. For a candidate correction curve ﾎｴ(s), the residual at a blind observation was approximately

$$r_i=\mathrm{GR}^{\mathrm{horizontal}}_i-\left[a\,\mathrm{GR}^{\mathrm{typewell}}!\left(\widehat{\mathrm{TVT}}_{\mathrm{V2},i}+\delta(s_i)\right)+b+d(s_i)\right].$$

where d(s) is a low-order drift term. A robust likelihood reduced the influence of spikes and bad GR intervals. The visible prefix therefore calibrated both the GR mapping and the effective noise level before any blind-interval correction was accepted.

6.3 Low-capacity correction prior

The correction was deliberately low-dimensional:

$$\delta(s)=B_{\mathrm{spline}}(s)\theta_s+B_{\mathrm{PCA}}(s)\theta_p.$$



B_spline: piecewise-linear basis with 8 knots, fixed to zero at the start;

B_PCA: the first 8 strictly OOF residual-shape components;

smoothness and amplitude priors prevented high-frequency path chasing;

at most 800 valid GR observations were used per well.


This low-capacity prior was important: the Bayesian layer could express broad stratigraphic drift and a small number of learned residual shapes, but it could not freely imitate noisy GR point by point.

6.4 Multi-start dynamic programming and posterior averaging

GR alignment is multi-modal: several TVT shifts can match similar-looking log patterns. A single optimizer start was therefore unsafe.

We generated coarse dynamic-programming starts over shifts from 竏・0 to +30 ft in 2-ft increments, using transition penalties {0.01, 0.05, 0.20}. Up to seven sufficiently distinct modes were refined with the original 21-point smoothed GR likelihood and combined using approximate posterior evidence. Duplicate converged modes were removed so that repeated starting points could not gain artificial posterior mass.

Thus, no matter how strongly the GR posterior disagreed, it could move the path by at most ﾂｱ4.5 ft. If a well had fewer than 20 usable GR observations or posterior fitting failed, the pipeline automatically fell back to the unchanged V2 prediction.

That cap was not merely defensive programming. It encoded our belief that the supervised/physical branch should set the global trajectory, while the Bayesian layer should make only a local geological correction. In other words, uncertainty affected both which modes were averaged and how much the final posterior could move the prediction.

The searched CV-best policy used weights 20/40/40, a ﾂｱ15 ft raw clip and output weight 0.525. It achieved a slightly lower aggregate score:

The aggressive policy was also worse on Reference folds 0 and 1 and on Strict fold 1. When policy selection itself was nested with leave-one-fold-out selection, the score deteriorated to 7.119409 / 7.551179. That was a clear warning that the apparent CV minimum included selection optimism.

Our final rule was simple:


  
Prefer the pre-specified policy that improves every fold under both protocols over the policy that wins only the repeatedly searched aggregate.


8. What the Bayesian analysis ruled out

LightGBM did not survive the Bayesian layer

A 70% CatBoost / 30% LightGBM-Huber base improved the V2-only score slightly. After applying the same Bayesian policies, however, it was effectively tied with or slightly worse than the Cat-only base. The extra model therefore added complexity without independent evidence of a stable gain.

Public leaderboard optimization was the wrong final objective

The Public-LB-best submission was excellent on the visible split, but its private score was much worse. Our final selection treated Public LB as diagnostic information窶馬ot a validation fold.

10. What we learned



Split at the natural entity level. In this competition the entity was the well, not the row.

Use physics to define the search space. Particle alignment, heel anchoring and structural fields made the learned task much easier.

Let uncertainty control correction size. The Bayesian layer was useful because it was conservative, multi-start and bounded.

Track selection bias, not only CV. A tiny aggregate improvement after a large search is not automatically the best final model.

Make final candidates genuinely different. Two branches that collapse after the same calibration do not provide leaderboard-shake protection.

Public LB is one sample. It should never replace a reproducible validation argument.


Conclusion

Our silver medal did not come from choosing the lowest number we could find. It came from deciding which number we trusted.

The selected solution combined a strong CatBoost/physics prior with a small, multi-modal and low-capacity Bayesian GR posterior. The Bayesian layer was decisive precisely because it was restrained: it preserved several plausible geological alignments, weighted them by evidence, and never moved the prior by more than ﾂｱ4.5 ft. We gave up a tiny amount of searched local CV and a large amount of Public-LB excitement in exchange for fold consistency, bootstrap support and lower selection bias. On the private leaderboard, that trade was worth exactly 1.000 RMSE and a silver medal.

Observing the data, the following characteristics were obtained:




The TVT movement within the well is virtually identical to the Z movement. The bedding planes remain almost unchanged, and it appears that the Z movement is reflected directly according to the relationship TVT = g - Z + datum. Therefore, T' = g' - Z' = constant - Z'. This means that the lateral curvature of the TVT is virtually identical to the Z curvature, and the prediction of the TVT slope is limited to a single constant in specific regions. The length over which the bedding plane slope g' remains constant appears to be approximately 300 ft.


Perfectly matching the typewell GR (GV) and horizontal GR (GH) is meaningless. Indeed, the TVT is not matched in this way either. However, statistics confirm that the acceptable range of discrepancies is not unlimited, and discrepancies exist in the form of a thick-tailed t-distribution.


Neighboring bedding planes can serve as good bedding plane priors and are good estimators of the g' described above.


Although very simple, it serves as a powerful method for reconstructing the correct path almost completely in most easy problems.

By calculating the accurate error statistics for all the observations above and inputting them into the HMM's emission and prior costs, we were able to make good estimates.

What you get from this post: a way to decide which leaderboard differences
are worth acting on. Two facts that look contradictory turn out to be true at
the same time, and seeing why sorted out a confusion I had been carrying for
weeks.



Two statements that both look right

Reading back through the CV-vs-LB threads, I kept finding both of these:



The public LB tracks CV almost perfectly.

The public LB is so noisy that reseeding alone moves you 0.2窶・.5 ft.


I assumed one of them had to be wrong. They are both correct 窶・they answer
different questions, and I had been applying the answer from one to the other.

The fact that separates them: the public well set is fixed

The public leaderboard is 26% of the test set. With roughly 200 hidden wells,
that is about 52 wells 窶・and crucially, it is the same 52 wells every time.

So if that particular slice happens to be harder than the 773-well training
population, that is not noise. It is a constant offset, and a constant
offset cancels in any comparison made on that same set.

That is exactly what the public data shows. In
discussion 719389,
yu4u (7th place) published five of their own CV / public-LB pairs. Fitting
them:










correlation
r = 0.999


slope
1.01 (essentially unit)


offset
+0.32 ft, constant


scatter about the fit
0.028 ft






The public set is harder by a near-constant amount. Their leaderboard ordering
reproduces their CV ordering to within three hundredths of a foot. On that
evidence, the public board is an extremely precise instrument 窶・for comparing
things measured on it.

So where does the noise everyone reports come from?

Not from sampling the wells 窶・that part is frozen. It comes from your own
pipeline being nondeterministic between runs. Reports in these threads:



byte-identical notebooks resubmitted, differing by ~0.5 ft
(discussion 701995)

reseeding one step of the algorithm moving 0.2窶・.3 ft (k256.dev, 18th, thread 719389)

a tightly seed-controlled pipeline showing 0.028 ft (yu4u's table above)


That range is a property of the submission, not of the leaderboard. It is also
the single cheapest thing you can shrink: the tighter your seeds, the finer the
differences the board can resolve for you.

So your resolution on the public board is essentially your own seed band. That
is measurable: submit the same notebook twice and see how far apart the scores
land. I hadn't done this, and it turned out to be the number I most needed.

The other question, where the noise is real

Now ask a different thing: will my public score hold up on the private set?

That is a fresh draw of wells, so the fixed offset no longer cancels and you
face genuine sampling error. For an RMSE over n independent units,

SE(RMSE) ~ RMSE / sqrt(2n)

At n ~ 52 wells and RMSE ~ 8, that is about 0.78 ft.

Two honest caveats about that number. It assumes roughly Gaussian errors, and
the unit has to be the well, not the row 窶・errors within a wellbore are
heavily correlated, so 14k rows do not buy you 14k units. If anything, both
corrections push the effective n down, making the true uncertainty larger
rather than smaller.



So an absolute public score is a weak estimate of a private one, and a gap
between two teams' absolute scores only means something when it is large
compared to that error bar. This is the part I had been reading far too
confidently.

Why "correlation dies below CV 6" fits this picture

Tucker Arrants (8th) reported losing CV窶鏑B correlation once CV dropped below
about 6, and several people report inversions like 竏・.9 CV for +0.1 LB.

That fits this picture. As a model improves, the differences being chased shrink
toward the seed band. Above it, the board ranks models cleanly; below it, what
moves is the pipeline's own randomness. On this reading the correlation isn't
breaking 窶・the measurement is running out of resolution.

What I'm taking from this



Measure the seed band. Submitting an identical notebook twice gives a
personal resolution limit, and anything below it is a coin flip.

Compare within one set at a time. CV against CV, LB against LB. The
+0.3-ish offset between them looks like a property of the well split rather
than a problem with a model.

Lean on CV for selection 窶・773 wells against ~52 is not a close call 窶・and
use the LB mainly to catch things that are structurally broken.

Be careful reading rank movement as skill movement in the dense band.
Mapping that ﾂｱ0.78 ft onto the current board, it spans an enormous number of
places, which says more about how crowded the band is than about any model in
it.




What this does not claim

I have not verified that the hidden test is ~200 wells; that follows from the
stated 26% and community estimates, and if it is materially different then the
0.78 ft scales with it. I also have not tested k256.dev's claim that the 773
wells split into two groups with different LB correlation 窶・that is their
observation, not mine, and it would refine this picture rather than contradict it.

Genuine thanks to everyone who posted their actual numbers in these threads 窶・
that is the only reason any of this is checkable rather than just a story. If
your own CV/LB pairs disagree with the picture above, I would really like to
see them.
